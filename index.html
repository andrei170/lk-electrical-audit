624 Pages. 618 Of Them Are The Same Page With The Town Name Swapped.

99.7%
identical — two of your town pages, word for word

You employ eleven people. Your website is written as though you are one man in a van, and it has not measured a single visit since 2023.

We took two of your pages at random — Preston and Blackpool — stripped the HTML and compared them word by word. 651 words each. The only difference is the sum on the spam-check box. The rest is the same page, 618 times.

Site read 18 September 2026 · Companies House record read the same day
Finding 1 — The Site Is Six Real Pages Wearing 618 Costumes
Pages in your published sitemap: 624 Real pages: 6 Contact Us, Commercial Property Rewires, Domestic Property Rewire, Electric Heating, Electric Testing & Inspection, Industrial Property Rewire Generated pages: 618 103 towns x 6 services = 618 Built by: Page Generator Pro (a WordPress plugin that mass-produces town pages) Word-for-word comparison, /electrical-installations-in-preston/ vs /electrical-installations-in-blackpool/ Words on each page: 651 and 651 Overlap after swapping the town name: 99.7% The only difference: the anti-spam sum (3 + 1 vs 13 + 6)
This tactic had a shelf life, and it expired

Mass-generated town pages worked around 2017. Google now filters near-duplicate pages hard, and a page with no heading, no photographs of that town and 651 words shared with 617 siblings is exactly what the filter is for. More importantly, none of it speaks to the customer you actually want — a site manager letting a rewire, not somebody searching "electrician in Bacup".

Finding 2 — A Third Of Your Website Advertises Work You Do Not Want
Your 624 pages, split by what they sell: Domestic property rewires 103 pages Electric heating 103 pages (largely a domestic product) 208 pages — a third of your site Commercial property rewires 103 pages Industrial property rewire 103 pages Electrical installations 103 pages Testing & inspection 103 pages
You told us you only take commercial work and you chase tenders

If that is right, a third of your website is built to attract the enquiry you least want — a homeowner wanting a house rewired in Bacup. Every one of those enquiries costs you a phone call, a polite no, and whatever time it took to work out it was not for you.

It is worse than wasted effort. A buyer letting a commercial contract who lands on your site sees domestic rewires and electric heating given equal billing with industrial work — and quietly concludes you are a domestic sparky who also does the occasional unit. That is the opposite of the impression a tender application needs.

Finding 3 — Nothing Has Been Measured Since 2023
Tracking found on your site: Universal Analytics UA-107786263-12 dead — Google stopped processing UA data on 1 July 2023 Google Analytics 4 not found Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads tag not found Meta pixel not found Call tracking not found
The tag is still in the page, and it has been collecting nothing for three years

Your site has been running for three years with the equivalent of an unplugged meter bolted to the wall. You cannot tell which of the 618 town pages has ever produced an enquiry, or whether any of them has. When you next decide whether the website is worth anything, there will be no figure to decide with.

What your homepage says, word for word: "you will receive the skills of a local, fully qualified electrician that has 20 years of experience" "the service will be conducted by a fully qualified electrician" Singular. Every time.
This is the single most expensive line on the site

A housing officer or a main contractor reading that page sees a sole trader and stops reading. You have grown from seven to eleven people in four filings and the website has not noticed. Team size, site capacity and the ability to hold a programme are what buys you the jobs you want — and they are nowhere on the site.

Finding 5 — The Form Cannot Tell A Consumer Unit From A Student Block
Every field on your contact form: Name yes Email yes Message yes Anti-spam sum yes Phone number not asked What the job is not asked Where it is not asked When it starts not asked Budget or size not asked
Four fields, one of which is a maths puzzle

Somebody with a twelve-unit HMO rewire and somebody wanting a socket moved arrive in the same inbox looking identical, and neither of them has left you a phone number. You are also reading enquiries at lkelectricalengineeringltd@gmail.com and publishing a mobile, 07580 711 512, as the only number on the site. For domestic work that is fine. For a commercial buyer it reads as a one-man band before they have read a word.

Finding 6 — The Small Things Google And Buyers Both Look At
Site description in your page code: "Just another WordPress site" (the WordPress factory default, never changed) Headings (H1) on the homepage: 0 Headings (H1) on the town pages: 0 Meta description on the homepage: none Homepage weight: ~4.9 MB HTML alone 236 KB Slider images 4.71 MB across 8 files Three single images over 1.2 MB each NAPIT logo shown: yes NAPIT registration number shown: nowhere on the site
None of these is fatal on its own. Together they are the whole impression.
  • "Just another WordPress site" is what your site tells Google it is about. It is the default text that ships with a blank install.
  • No H1 anywhere — the one heading a search engine weighs most is missing from all 624 pages.
  • Nearly 5 MB on a phone on a Heysham 4G signal is several seconds of white screen before anything appears.
  • An accreditation logo with no number cannot be checked. On a tender return, a logo without a registration number is worth nothing.
Finding 7 — The Work You Actually Want Is Let On Frameworks, And They Are Public
One live route we could verify, on your doorstep
  • Lancashire County Council, Mechanical & Electrical Works Partnering Framework. Estimated at £18,000,000 excluding VAT, running 1 September 2025 to 31 August 2029, work allocated to providers on a rotational basis. The notice is explicitly marked suitable for small and medium-sized enterprises.
And one that shows why you want to be visible before they open

Great Places Housing Group's repairs and maintenance notice carried a lot for Electrical — Lancashire (North). It is marked cancelled: no contracts will be awarded under that lot. We are showing you it precisely because it went away. These come round on a cycle, and the firms that get asked are the ones already known and already looking the part when the notice appears — not the ones who start building a case for themselves the week it opens.

What a buyer needs to see, and cannot

Named projects. Sectors. Team size. Accreditation numbers. Insurance levels. A case study with a photograph of the job. Your site has none of these. It has 618 town pages and a Gmail address.

What We Would Actually Do
  • Turn the meter back on. GA4, a proper tag, and call tracking, so that within a month you know which pages and which sources produce enquiries — and which of the 618 produce nothing.
  • Rewrite the site as an eleven-person contractor. Team, capacity, sectors, accreditation numbers, insurance levels, named projects with photographs of your own work.
  • Replace the form with a qualifier. Job type, property type, location, size, timing and a phone number — so a student block and a socket move never look the same again.
  • Cut the 618 down to the towns you genuinely work and write them properly, then put the effort into service and sector pages a buyer will actually read.
  • Build the credibility a framework application needs, so that when the next Lancashire notice opens you are not starting from a Gmail address.

Eleven people, and a website that says "a fully qualified electrician".

The business grew. The site did not. That is the whole gap.
